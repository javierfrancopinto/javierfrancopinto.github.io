Research

I work on two lines of research at the Institute of Economics, FCEA. The first is in macroeconomics and fiscal policy, on the distributional consequences of fiscal consolidations. The second is in inequality and poverty, on how adverse labor shocks (particularly a household head losing their job) affect household finances and access to credit. Both bring macro-level policy analysis together with micro-level evidence on how households absorb economic shocks.

Master's Thesis · Expected Defense Oct. 2026

“Does Austerity Redistribute?”

Uses a pooled sample of OECD and Latin American and Caribbean economies, with consolidation episodes identified through the narrative approach and analyzed with local projections. The question is whether the distributional effects of fiscal consolidations depend more on a country's quality of government (a composite measure of state capacity) than on region, and whether tax-based and expenditure-based adjustments differ once state capacity is taken into account.

Research Assistant Project · Ongoing

Interest over Interests: Analysis of Uruguayan Household Debt

Uses individual-level administrative data to trace household finances after a household head loses their job, looking at whether the financial system cushions or amplifies the income shock, and how that shapes access to credit.
